# QUIC Requirement Gaps

This ledger tracks open questions, ambiguities, and follow-up decisions for QUIC requirement work.

## Open Gaps

- `interop-all-upstream-client-nonhandshake-xquic-liveness` is open as the remaining local client-role all-upstream `transfer`/`keyupdate`/`chacha20` residual after the May 19, 2026 refresh and is now trace-owned by `REQ-QUIC-INT-0029`, `ARC-QUIC-INT-0023`, `WI-QUIC-INT-0023`, and `VER-QUIC-INT-0023`. The generic stream-data liveness bug is closed by the focused ordered-read wake-up proof and by live quic-go `transfer`, `chacha20`, and `keyupdate` reruns. The ACK scheduler now has focused proof that the two-packet delayed-ACK threshold re-arms after a prior ACK while still delaying after only one new ack-eliciting packet. The broad client-role run under `.artifacts/interop-runner/all-upstream-streamdata-keyupdate-local/client/20260519-121832639-client-chrome` now passes `transfer` and `keyupdate` for 15 of 16 upstream server-capable peers, passes `chacha20` for 14 of 16, and reports `chacha20` unknown/unsupported for `mvfst` and `go-x-net`. The only failed peer cluster is `xquic`, where all three cells time out before the first generated response body completes. That preserved May 19 run shows `transfer`, `keyupdate`, and `chacha20` all timing out after 35,400 bytes at the response-stream FIN boundary. A focused `xquic` rerun after the ACK-threshold topoff timed out under `.artifacts/interop-runner/debug-client-transfer-xquic-after-ack-threshold/20260519-140609871-client-chrome` after 35,400 bytes; a fresh May 22, 2026 rerun under `.artifacts/interop-runner/debug-client-transfer-xquic-current-after-docker-cleanup/20260522-221632093-client-chrome` timed out after 14,174 bytes of a 2,097,152-byte first generated response body, while the simulator captured client/server packet paths with zero interface drops. A fresh May 23, 2026 rerun under `.artifacts/interop-runner/debug-client-transfer-xquic-live-refresh/20260523-161053951-client-chrome` failed after the client read 15,355 bytes, and the xquic server log still only decrypts/processes client short-header packets `0..3`. The May 23 captured-packet proofs open client-to-xquic 1-RTT packets from that rerun, verify ACK coverage through xquic response packet 17 plus a MAX_DATA frame, and verify an alternating repeated MAX_DATA/MAX_STREAM_DATA sequence through client packet 28 with increasing limits. A bounded artifact comparison found both simulator pcaps contain decryptable client short-header packets `0..40`, while xquic's own server log only decrypts/processes client short-header packets `0..3`, so the next slice should prioritize post-send drain, pacing wakeup, peer delivery, xquic event-loop packet consumption, and stream-drain behavior before changing ACK generation, packet protection, or local credit publication. The fresh live rerun also reaches `xqc_process_read_streams|stream_read_notify|flag:66` after the request stream is fully received, and the server-side response burst runs through packet 22 before `xqc_send_packet_pacer_allows|pacing blocked` at `stream_offset:16536` prevents the transfer from draining to FIN. The local runner helper now reports that preserved packet/key material confirms valid post-burst ACK traffic while xquic's server-log short-header packet-processing ceiling remains a non-promoting failure diagnostic while keeping the run failed. Treat this as a reduced xquic-specific liveness investigation; it is not HTTP/3, migration, server-role non-handshake, or support-readiness proof.

- `interop-all-upstream-client-nonhandshake-xquic-liveness-fresh-rerun` captures the fresh May 23, 2026 `keyupdate`/`chacha20` rerun under `.artifacts/interop-runner/debug-client-keyupdate-chacha20-xquic-live-refresh/20260523-192303830-client-chrome`, which also failed after the client read 15,355 bytes. That keeps the newest runtime evidence aligned across transfer, keyupdate, and chacha20 and confirms the residual still belongs to the shared xquic non-handshake ceiling rather than a transfer-only artifact.
- The local runner helper now also has a shared-ceiling fallback for `keyupdate` and `chacha20` when the server log does not expose the deeper packet markers, so the non-promoting diagnostic stays useful even when the broad server-side packet breadcrumbs are absent.
- `non-http3-interop-suite-inventory` remains open for cells that have not been promoted by their own proof slice. `rebind-port` and `rebind-addr` are RFC 9000 migration/path-validation interop cells, not a different RFC, but they still need dedicated live runner evidence and inventory promotion before they can move out of prerequisite-blocked status. The current local `rebind-port` artifact at `artifacts/interop-runner/rebind-proof/20260520-165752528-server-nginx` shows no client packet from the second rebound external port. The prior local `rebind-addr` artifact at `artifacts/interop-runner/rebind-proof/20260520-171906828-server-nginx` completed the download but exposed that the repo-owned analyzer shim was checking server path changes from the client trace; the helper now uses the server trace for that check. A fresh May 22, 2026 rerun under `.artifacts/interop-runner/rebind-addr-server-trace-shim/20260522-223326878-server-nginx` still failed before analyzer post-check: the client timed out after the second rebound address while packets to the previous binding were dropped. Keep both rebinding cells prerequisite-blocked until stable live transfer and path-validation evidence exists. Corruption and ECN cells remain outside this helper-supported inventory until their own proof slice promotes them.

## Future RFC Planning Gaps


- `http3-adapter-boundary` is partially narrowed by `REQ-QUIC-INT-0030`, `ARC-QUIC-INT-0024`, `WI-QUIC-INT-0024`, and `VER-QUIC-INT-0024` for the bounded QUIC interop-runner `TESTCASE=http3` endpoint adapter, by `REQ-QUIC-INT-0031`, `ARC-QUIC-INT-0025`, `WI-QUIC-INT-0025`, and `VER-QUIC-INT-0025` for the advisory external HTTP/3 peer harness/reporting surface, by `REQ-QUIC-INT-0032`, `ARC-QUIC-INT-0026`, `WI-QUIC-INT-0026`, and `VER-QUIC-INT-0026` for advisory h3spec server conformance triage, and by protocol-owned `REQ-QUIC-RFC9114-S4-0001`, `ARC-QUIC-RFC9114-0001`, `WI-QUIC-RFC9114-0001`, and `VER-QUIC-RFC9114-0001` for deterministic low-level malformed-sequence tests. Broader `rfc9114.txt` sections `3`, `6.2.1`-`6.2.2`, `7.2.4`-`7.2.4.1`, `7`, and `9` remain an open planning gap for production HTTP/3 adapter behavior beyond these harness cells. Pressure class: `HTTP/3 adapter`, `stream adapter`, and `diagnostics/manageability`. Allocation-sensitive seams: effect emission and stream delivery across the transport boundary, especially `QuicConnectionRuntimeEventModels` and `QuicListenerHost`.

- `qpack-stream-state-boundary` is partially narrowed by the bounded `TESTCASE=http3` runner adapter, which uses the standalone QPACK module through the HTTP/3 layer without dynamic table emission in the runner path, and by the h3spec triage parser that maps QPACK failures back to RFC 9204 sections without promoting them as fixed. Broader `rfc9204.txt` sections `2`-`5` and `7.1.1` remain an open planning gap for production QPACK stream-state capture, dynamic synchronization, and restore behavior. Pressure class: `stream adapter` and `HTTP/3 adapter`. Allocation-sensitive seams: stream-state capture and restore, especially `QuicConnectionStreamSnapshot` and `QuicConnectionStreamState`.

- `websockets-http3-extended-connect` is a planning gap for `rfc9220.txt` section `3`. Pressure class: `HTTP/3 adapter` and `stream adapter`. Current action is architecture planning only unless support is explicitly committed later. Allocation-sensitive seams: HTTP/3 stream delivery and adapter effect emission above the core transport runtime.

- `http-datagrams-and-capsules` is a planning gap for `rfc9297.txt` sections `2.1`-`2.2`, `3.1`-`3.5`, and `5.1`-`5.4`. Pressure class: `datagram adapter`, `stream adapter`, and `HTTP/3 adapter`. Current action is architecture planning only unless support is explicitly committed later. Allocation-sensitive seams: outbound datagram ownership and capsule/stream delivery, especially `QuicConnectionRuntimeEventModels.QuicConnectionSendDatagramEffect`, `QuicConnectionRuntime.SendDatagram`, `QuicListenerHost.SendDatagram`, and `QuicConnectionStreamSnapshot`.

- `connect-udp` is a planning gap for `rfc9298.txt` sections `3`-`5`. Pressure class: `datagram adapter`, `HTTP/3 adapter`, and `stream adapter`. Current action is architecture planning only unless support is explicitly committed later. Allocation-sensitive seams: UDP payload ownership and HTTP Datagrams bridging, especially the same outbound datagram path used by `QuicConnectionRuntime` and `QuicListenerHost`.

- `connect-ip-masque` is a planning gap for `rfc9484.txt` sections `4.1`, `4.7`, `6`, `7`, `10`, `12.1`-`12.4`. Pressure class: `datagram adapter`, `HTTP/3 adapter`, and `diagnostics/manageability`. Current action is architecture planning only unless support is explicitly committed later. Allocation-sensitive seams: outbound datagram ownership, packet handling, and MTU-sensitive proxy flow, especially `QuicConnectionRuntimeEventModels.QuicConnectionSendDatagramEffect` and the adapter boundary above `QuicConnectionRuntime`.

- `dns-over-quic` is a planning gap for `rfc9250.txt` sections `3.1`-`5.8` and `8.1`-`8.2`. Pressure class: `DNS/discovery edge`, `stream adapter`, and `transport-core`. Current action is architecture planning only unless support is explicitly committed later. Allocation-sensitive seams: stream delivery and handshake/resumption state, especially `QuicConnectionStreamSnapshot` and the `QuicConnectionRuntime` TLS/transport bridge flow.

- `encrypted-dns-discovery-and-provisioning` is a planning gap for `rfc9461.txt` sections `3.1`, `4.1`-`4.3`, `rfc9463.txt` sections `3.1`-`3.4`, `4.1`-`4.2`, `5.1`-`5.2`, and `6.1`-`6.2`, and `rfc9464.txt` sections `3.1`-`3.2`, `4.1`-`4.2`, and `5`. Pressure class: `DNS/discovery edge`. Current action is architecture planning only unless support is explicitly committed later. Allocation-sensitive seams: no QUIC hot-path seam; keep resolver/configuration data as scalar snapshots that enter the core only at bootstrap or endpoint selection time.

- `manageability-and-applicability-guidance` is a planning gap for `rfc9308.txt` sections `2`, `3.1`-`3.2`, `4.1`-`4.5`, `8.1`, `9`, `11.1`-`11.3`, `13`-`15`, and `rfc9312.txt` sections `2.1`-`4.10`. Pressure class: `diagnostics/manageability` and `interop/test-only`. Current action is architecture planning only unless support is explicitly committed later. Allocation-sensitive seams: diagnostics emission and packet/version observation, especially `QuicConnectionRuntime.DiagnosticsSink`, runtime event emission, and packet inspection helpers.
