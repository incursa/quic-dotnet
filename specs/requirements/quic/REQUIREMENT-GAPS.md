# QUIC Requirement Gaps

This ledger tracks open questions, ambiguities, and follow-up decisions for QUIC requirement work.

## Open Gaps

- `congestion-controller-selector-prerequisite` is open for the internal construction-time congestion-controller selector: the repository already has the RFC 9002 NewReno requirement homes and the RFC 8085 alternate-controller guidance, but the selector itself still needs a canonical CRT slice that fixes default NewReno, immutable per-path choice, internal-only CUBIC, deterministic monotonic time, and path-local ACK/loss/ECN/persistent-congestion and PMTU/MSS invariants before implementation.
- `interop-all-upstream-client-nonhandshake-xquic-liveness` is open as the remaining local client-role all-upstream `transfer`/`keyupdate`/`chacha20` residual after the May 19, 2026 refresh and is now trace-owned by `REQ-QUIC-INT-0029`, `ARC-QUIC-INT-0023`, `WI-QUIC-INT-0023`, and `VER-QUIC-INT-0023`. The generic stream-data liveness bug is closed by the focused ordered-read wake-up proof and by live quic-go `transfer`, `chacha20`, and `keyupdate` reruns. The ACK scheduler now has focused proof that the two-packet delayed-ACK threshold re-arms after a prior ACK while still delaying after only one new ack-eliciting packet. The broad client-role run under `.artifacts/interop-runner/all-upstream-streamdata-keyupdate-local/client/20260519-121832639-client-chrome` now passes `transfer` and `keyupdate` for 15 of 16 upstream server-capable peers, passes `chacha20` for 14 of 16, and reports `chacha20` unknown/unsupported for `mvfst` and `go-x-net`. The only failed peer cluster is `xquic`, where all three cells time out before the first generated response body completes. That preserved May 19 run shows `transfer`, `keyupdate`, and `chacha20` all timing out after 35,400 bytes at the response-stream FIN boundary. A focused `xquic` rerun after the ACK-threshold topoff timed out under `.artifacts/interop-runner/debug-client-transfer-xquic-after-ack-threshold/20260519-140609871-client-chrome` after 35,400 bytes; a fresh May 22, 2026 rerun under `.artifacts/interop-runner/debug-client-transfer-xquic-current-after-docker-cleanup/20260522-221632093-client-chrome` timed out after 14,174 bytes of a 2,097,152-byte first generated response body, while the simulator captured client/server packet paths with zero interface drops. A fresh May 23, 2026 rerun under `.artifacts/interop-runner/debug-client-transfer-xquic-live-refresh/20260523-161053951-client-chrome` failed after the client read 15,355 bytes, and the xquic server log still only decrypts/processes client short-header packets `0..3`. The May 23 captured-packet proofs open client-to-xquic 1-RTT packets from that rerun, verify ACK coverage through xquic response packet 17 plus a MAX_DATA frame, and verify an alternating repeated MAX_DATA/MAX_STREAM_DATA sequence through client packet 28 with increasing limits. A bounded artifact comparison found both simulator pcaps contain decryptable client short-header packets `0..40`, while xquic's own server log only decrypts/processes client short-header packets `0..3`, so the next slice should prioritize post-send drain, pacing wakeup, peer delivery, xquic event-loop packet consumption, and stream-drain behavior before changing ACK generation, packet protection, or local credit publication. The fresh live rerun also reaches `xqc_process_read_streams|stream_read_notify|flag:66` after the request stream is fully received, and the server-side response burst runs through packet 22 before `xqc_send_packet_pacer_allows|pacing blocked` at `stream_offset:16536` prevents the transfer from draining to FIN. The local runner helper now reports that preserved packet/key material confirms valid post-burst ACK traffic while xquic's server-log short-header packet-processing ceiling remains a non-promoting failure diagnostic while keeping the run failed. Treat this as a reduced xquic-specific liveness investigation; it is not HTTP/3, migration, server-role non-handshake, or support-readiness proof.

- `interop-all-upstream-client-nonhandshake-xquic-liveness-fresh-rerun` captures the fresh May 23, 2026 `keyupdate`/`chacha20` rerun under `.artifacts/interop-runner/debug-client-keyupdate-chacha20-xquic-live-refresh/20260523-192303830-client-chrome`, which also failed after the client read 15,355 bytes. The June 18, 2026 refresh under `.artifacts/interop-runner/debug-client-xquic-refresh/20260618-085552662-client-chrome` reran `transfer`, `keyupdate`, and `chacha20` together and all three failed with the same 15,355-byte response-stream FIN timeout; the xquic server logs reached `xqc_process_read_streams|stream_read_notify|flag:66`, scheduled response bytes through `stream_offset:16536`, and then reported `xqc_send_packet_pacer_allows|pacing blocked`, while the runner report preserved all three cells as failed. That keeps the newest runtime evidence aligned across transfer, keyupdate, and chacha20 and classifies the residual as non-promoting external xquic liveness evidence rather than a transfer-only artifact or local ACK/protection candidate.
- The local runner helper now also has a shared-ceiling fallback for `keyupdate` and `chacha20` when the server log does not expose the deeper packet markers, so the non-promoting diagnostic stays useful even when the broad server-side packet breadcrumbs are absent.
- `non-http3-interop-suite-inventory` remains open for cells that have not been promoted by their own proof slice. `rebind-port` and `rebind-addr` are RFC 9000 migration/path-validation interop cells, not a different RFC. `rebind-port` is promoted by the June 11, 2026 direct runner artifact at `artifacts/interop-runner/20260611-085254961-direct-rebind-port-cid-warmup-tools-patched`, which records `quic-go`/`chrome` `rebind-port` as `succeeded`, a 10 MiB managed transfer completion, two observed server paths, and qlog-backed PATH_CHALLENGE/PATH_RESPONSE evidence for the rebound port. `rebind-addr` is promoted by the June 11, 2026 direct runner artifact at `artifacts/interop-runner/20260611-090616967-direct-rebind-addr-cid-warmup-tools-patched`, which records `quic-go`/`chrome` `rebind-addr` as `succeeded`, a 10 MiB managed transfer completion, three observed server paths, and qlog-backed PATH_CHALLENGE/PATH_RESPONSE evidence for the rebound addresses. `transfercorruption` is promoted by the June 11, 2026 helper-backed runner artifact at `artifacts/interop-runner/20260611-094544073-client-chrome`, which records quic-go/chrome `transfercorruption` as `succeeded` under moderate packet corruption. `handshakecorruption` remains an explicit prerequisite-blocked inventory cell rather than a generic missing entry; the June 11, 2026 helper-backed diagnostic at `artifacts/interop-runner/20260611-092726774-client-chrome` mapped through the upstream multiconnect-derived corruption flow and failed with local client `FRAME_ENCODING_ERROR`/unknown frame type `0x23de` evidence. ECN remains outside the helper-supported inventory because the managed socket receive path uses `Socket.ReceiveMessageFromAsync`/`SocketReceiveMessageFromResult`, which exposes packet information for the destination address but not the IP_TOS or IPV6_TCLASS ancillary ECN bits required to count received ECT(0), ECT(1), or ECN-CE packets. The 2026-06-19 no-op receive seam keeps this limitation explicit through `QuicSocketEcnControl.TryGetReceivedEcnCounts` and the endpoint/listener receive loops while preserving current behavior. The runtime still accepts explicit `QuicEcnCounts`, and ACK_ECN helper tests cover supplied counts, but live local ECN promotion needs a native/control-message receive path or equivalent platform-specific proof before the inventory cell can move beyond platform-blocked.
